## License comparison

- **Apache-2.0**: permissive, includes an express patent license and contributor patent termination; recommended for a modular research project that may receive commercial and non-commercial extensions.
- **MIT**: shortest and simplest permissive license, but provides less explicit patent language and fewer contributor protections.
- **MPL-2.0**: file-level copyleft; modifications to MPL-covered files remain open while allowing larger combined works under other terms. It gives stronger reciprocity but adds compliance complexity.

The prepared default is Apache-2.0. This is a project-level recommendation, not legal advice.

## Difference from a conventional LLM agent

An ordinary LLM agent usually begins with a pretrained model, a task prompt, tools, and session or external memory. This architecture treats the LLM as an optional provider and makes Individual continuity, body ownership, artificial physiology, developmental learning, lifecycle boundaries, and concept formation first-class research problems. It does not claim that a prompt plus memory equals an individual.

## Relation to Developmental Robotics

Developmental Robotics provides important research on embodied learning, sensorimotor development, exploration, and developmental stages. This project is compatible with that field and seeks to place those mechanisms inside a broader lifecycle framework that also specifies identity, birth boundaries, artificial physiology, provider independence, data provenance, and long-term continuity. It does not claim to replace Developmental Robotics or to invent its methods.

## How to publish without source code

Describe the repository honestly as an **Architecture-First Public Release** and label every item as Architecture, Research Goal, Planned, Experimental, or Implemented. Do not add decorative demos or imply that a complete system exists. The specification, diagrams, contracts, roadmap, and explicit limits are valid first-release assets.
